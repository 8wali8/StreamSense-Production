# Real sponsor logo detection: scope

Scoped 2026-09-20 against `origin/main` at `e120ea3` (`deals/02-edit-deal` merged as #74) from the PRD "Real sponsor logo detection" (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/750905df-a71e-423c-a893-46cd33a26b2e). The PRD says what "real and correct" means from the streamer's and the sponsor's side; this document says what that costs in the code, in what order, and where the risk sits. Branch prefix `logo/`; one note per branch under `branches/`, as for `cloud/` and `hardening/`.

## Where the PRD lands today

What the detector plugs into is exactly as the PRD describes, and the seam is narrow. The sentence in bold is the thing each requirement needs that does not exist yet.

| PRD | What exists | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| R1 the deal carries the logo | `deals` table and `DealCreateRequest` hold terms only; `deals/02-edit-deal` (merged as #74) added `PUT`/`DELETE` and turned `NewDealForm` into `DealForm` with an edit mode. analytics-service has no object storage client; MinIO holds frames only. | **A logo per deal (up to two), stored, served to the console, and reachable by ml-engine.** The upload belongs in that edit form. |
| R2 what counts as on screen | analytics credits 10 s per accepted detection (`confidence ≥ 0.50`), prominence `0.5 + min(0.5, area × 10)` from the box area; the gateway collapses accepted detections closer than 30 s into a segment. None of this changes. | **A detector that answers "the deal's logo is here, at this box" or "it is not", instead of a hash of the frame.** The 30 s gap rule already absorbs one missed sample without splitting a segment, so a miss costs 10 s of credit, not a broken timeline. |
| R3 what the report shows | Tiles, timeline, best and weakest, deal totals all read the buckets; the timeline's selected moment links to the VOD at its offset. Frames are served by video-capture-service at `/api/video/capture/frame?frameRef=` (bearer token required) and shown only in the ops segmentation preview. | **The sampled frame with the box, per segment, in the report and on a share link** (a share tab has no bearer token and only `POST /graphql` is open to it). The live strip already polls `sessionSummary` every 30 s, inside the one-minute target. |
| R4 verifiability for the operator | `/ml/info` lists backends but is not routed through the gateway; the ops sponsor panel shows the last 20 raw detections with model version, box, and frame ref. | **A detector pill naming the backend and version; a per-session list of sampled frames and outcomes; production refusing the placeholder.** |
| R5 imports get the same treatment | An import replays frames at the live interval through the same topic and consumer; ids are deterministic so a re-import counts once. | Nothing new for correctness. **Priority**: an import publishes about a frame per second against one every 10 s live, into the same topic and the same bulkhead of four, so a long import can delay a live stream's detections. |
| Quality bar | `tools/ml/compare_backends.py` covers sentiment and relevance only. There is no labelled frame set and no way to score a detector. | **An evaluation harness and labelled fixtures**, and the controlled streams the PRD names. |
| "Tracking unavailable, not placeholder numbers" | video-service's fallback publishes `sponsor: UNKNOWN`, `modelVersion: fallback`, confidence 0; analytics counts it per bucket as `fallback_detection_count` and it feeds the `sponsorQualityRisk` factor. | **The report has to say "on-screen tracking was unavailable for N minutes" from those counts**, and a frame with no logo found must not be counted as a low-confidence detection the way it would be today. |

Two facts about the current contract shape every branch has to respect:

- `/ml/sponsor` always answers with a sponsor. There is no "nothing here". The detection event schema requires `sponsor` (min length 1) and `additionalProperties: false`, and changes must be backward compatible (add optional fields only).
- analytics keys exposure by the sponsor **name** on the event and matches it to the deal's sponsor case-insensitively at report time. The detector must therefore stamp the deal's sponsor name on every event, and a later rename of the deal's sponsor leaves old events under the old name (an existing property, not made worse here; carrying `dealId` on the event makes it fixable later).

## Decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Detector | Feature matching in ml-engine: keypoints on the frame once, matched against each logo variant's keypoints, accepted only with a geometric verification (RANSAC homography with an inlier floor) and a second check of the warped region against the logo (normalised correlation). OpenCV headless, pinned. Model version `logo-match-v1`. | A streamer's overlay is the logo file itself, rendered at some scale and position; that is the case feature matching is best at, and it fails closed: no consistent geometry, no detection. It runs in well under a second per 1080p frame on CPU with no GPU, needs no training data at deal creation, is deterministic (the same recording imported twice gives the same numbers, acceptance step 4), and adds no torch or transformers change, so the pinned ML stack and its comparison procedure are untouched. Physical objects and text (non-goals) are exactly what it will miss, and the report will say zero rather than guess. |
| Where the logo lives | A `deal_logos` table in analytics-service (id, deal, variant, object key, content type, sha256, width, height, uploaded at) and the bytes in a `streamsense-logos` MinIO bucket written by analytics-service with the frame-storage credentials. | ml-engine already reads `s3://` artifacts with a size bound and has the network path to MinIO; a logo is one more artifact. analytics-service owns deals, so it owns the logo's row and its upload route. Keeping bytes out of Postgres keeps deal reads light. |
| Who resolves streamer to logo | video-service asks analytics-service for the channel's current deal and logos (`GET /api/analytics/deals/current?streamer=`), cached per channel for 60 s, and passes `sponsor`, `dealId`, `logoId`, and `logoRefs` in the `/ml/sponsor` request. With no logo it publishes an outcome `NO_LOGO` without calling ml-engine. | video-service already orchestrates the frame's journey; ml-engine stays stateless and deal-agnostic. Not calling ml-engine for channels without a logo is the single biggest CPU saving on the VM. |
| "Which logo" for a frame | The logo in effect at processing time, live or import, recorded on the event as `logoId`. Earlier reports are never recomputed. | The PRD's rule ("from that moment on"); a re-run action is a later branch if asked. |
| Outcome on the event | New optional `outcome` on `SponsorDetectionEvent`: `DETECTED`, `NOT_DETECTED`, `NO_LOGO`, `UNAVAILABLE`; `sponsor` is the deal's sponsor for the first three (`UNKNOWN` for unavailable, as today); confidence 0 and a zero box when not detected. Every sampled frame still yields one event, as now. | Additive, so old consumers keep working; analytics can tell "looked and did not find" from "could not look" from "was not asked"; the ops list of sampled frames comes for free from what video-service already persists. Volume is unchanged: one event per frame today too. |
| Frame proof for the sponsor | The gateway mints short-lived HMAC-signed frame URLs (`GET /frames/<signed>` proxied to video-capture-service) and a new query `exposureFrames(sessionId, sponsor, from, to, limit)` returns a segment's samples with box and URL; allowed in share mode and narrowed to the shared deal like `sponsorMoments`. The timeline loads it for the selected segment only. | An `<img>` cannot send the share header, and putting every sample into `sponsorMoments` would make a four-hour stream's timeline carry 1,400 entries. The gateway already holds an HMAC secret. |
| Live over import | Imports publish to a new `stream.video.frames.import` topic (same schema), consumed by a second listener in video-service with its own, smaller bulkhead; ml-engine bounds concurrent sponsor calls with a semaphore. Detection time per frame is recorded on the import status. | A second topic is the only change that guarantees a live frame never waits behind an import's backlog; everything else about the import path stays. The budget question (10 min per hour) is the owner's, and the number to decide it with is what the status will show. |
| Placeholder | `STREAMSENSE_SPONSOR_BACKEND=stub|logo-match`; the prod overlay sets `logo-match`, the deploy script's verify step reads `/ml/info` and fails on `stub`, and the ops panel shows any `stub-*` model version in red. Tests and the demo export keep the stub. | "Production never runs the placeholder" has to be enforced by something, not remembered. |
| Not in this scope | A brand library, OCR, physical objects, more than one sponsor per deal, changing the exposure arithmetic, recomputing old streams, frame retention policy. | The PRD's non-goals, plus retention, which is a storage question the current 100 GB disk does not force yet (about 100 MB of frames per channel-hour). |

## Ground rules

- CLAUDE.md conventions throughout: settings fields not `os.getenv`; every HTTP client bounded; schema change in the same commit as the event class with the compatibility check green; `docs/contracts/sponsor-pipeline.md` and `deals.md` updated in the branch that changes them; GraphQL types regenerated; a branch note with a verification table and hand checks.
- The logo upload lives in `DealForm` and the deal page that `deals/02-edit-deal` (#74) delivered; nothing about deals is rebuilt.
- No new external service. OpenCV is a library, not a service; nothing leaves the VM.
- The false-detection asymmetry is a design constraint, not a tuning target: every threshold defaults to the strict side and the harness reports false minutes first.

## Known risks

- **Small and compressed logos.** Feature matching needs texture; a 40-pixel-wide wordmark on a 1080p frame after Twitch's encoding may not yield enough keypoints. The size floor is measured on the fixtures in 03 and written into the deal page's guidance; below it the product says so rather than guessing. If the floor is too high for real overlays, the fallback lever is a second-stage verifier on candidate regions (a small CNN embedding through the existing torch stack), which would then bring the comparison procedure into play.
- **Look-alikes.** Another brand's overlay, a game HUD element, or a wordmark in the same font. The geometric check and the correlation check are both there for this; the harness's look-alike frames are the regression test, and the acceptance stream with no overlay is the field test.
- **CPU on the VM.** One `e2-standard-8` runs eight JVMs, Whisper, sentiment, relevance, and now matching. The design keeps matching to channels with a logo and to one keypoint pass per frame; 04 measures the rest. If live detection cannot hold the 10 s cadence with an import running, the semaphore and the import bulkhead are the knobs, and the owner decides the budget.
- **Frames on a share link.** A sponsor sees the streamer's actual screen at that moment. That is the product's promise, but the URLs must expire, must resolve only to frames inside the shared deal's sessions, and must not be guessable. 05's review is that check.
- **SVG.** A logo often arrives as SVG. Accepting it means rasterising server-side and refusing scripts and external references; refusing it means a streamer converts first. Refuse in 01, ask the owner (open question below).
- **Storage growth.** Frames are kept forever today; the frame proof makes deleting them a product decision. Not in this scope; noted for a retention branch.

## Open questions for the owner

The PRD's five stand (two variants or one; recompute on logo change; box or no box; the size floor; who the real streamer is). Three more from the code:

1. **SVG uploads.** Refuse in the first release, or rasterise?
2. **The demo snapshot.** Once the replay alias detects a real Red Bull logo, re-export the demo from it so the public page carries real numbers, or keep the stub snapshot until launch?
3. **Frames on the share link.** Full-resolution frame, or a downscaled copy? Full resolution is the more convincing proof and the larger exposure of the streamer's screen.
